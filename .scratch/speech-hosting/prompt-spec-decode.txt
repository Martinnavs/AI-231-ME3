CONTEXT. We run a voice agent. Speech-to-text (STT) text feeds an LLM. STT is NVIDIA Parakeet-TDT 0.6B v2 (int8, sherpa-onnx, CPU, NOT streaming: it decodes a finished audio buffer; ~280 ms + 72 ms per second of audio). To cut latency we do "early decode" (speculative decoding of the turn): when a voice-activity pause of ~200 ms is seen, we start the full-model decode on the audio so far, in the background, before the turn is confirmed at ~500 ms. If speech resumes, or the energy of a soft word ending reappears, the early decode is cancelled and we decode the whole turn later. If not cancelled, its text is reused as the final transcript.

THE PROBLEM. The early result is less accurate than a decode made after the full end-of-turn pause. Measured on labelled clips: short commands (3 s or less) WER 4.9% -> 7.4%; on longer sentences and on a 25-turn live session, no change. Cause we found: Parakeet's output depends on the audio around the speech. An early decode sees only ~0.2 s of trailing silence where the full decode sees 0.4+ s; that changes about 1 word in 10 on short commands (often the last word, or entity names). Energy-based pause detection also fires inside quiet word endings (a first version had WER 18.4% until we cancelled on any rise above 1.5x the noise floor). Early text matches the full decode in 89% of turns (75% on short commands). We also run a phonetic name-repair step and hotword biasing after decoding.
