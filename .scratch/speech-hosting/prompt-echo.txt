OUR SITUATION. A full-duplex English voice agent that must let the user interrupt ("barge in") while the agent is speaking. Setups: (1) a laptop with its own speakers and microphone, (2) a Raspberry Pi 4/5 with a USB speakerphone, (3) a headset. Hard rule: no model runs on the Pi except Silero VAD (so no neural echo canceller on the Pi). Python. Speech-to-text keeps listening while the agent talks; the text-to-speech output is known to us exactly (we have the samples we play and the words being spoken).
WHAT WE MEASURED. Without echo cancellation the agent's own voice leaks into the microphone and a VAD flags 91-92% of blocks as speech; Silero VAD is worst because the agent's voice IS speech. In a simulation with the leak at -18, -12, -6 dB, every software detector (loudness, Silero, and combinations) made the agent interrupt itself, sometimes in loops of 30+ self-interruptions. An "ignore it if the heard words are the agent's own words" filter cut the loops 5-8x but not the first false stop. On this laptop PipeWire's WebRTC echo-cancel module brought false VAD triggers to 0% of blocks (but the first play still leaked about 11 dB above the room floor, and the module lowered the room noise floor by 12-17 dB, which may hurt recognition accuracy; untested).
