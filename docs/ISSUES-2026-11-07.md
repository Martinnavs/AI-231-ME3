## C. Evaluation gaps
- Barge-in, `interrupted` and `bot_stopped` marks are not unit-tested; the first live runs produced barge-in data, so they work in practice.
- Context size per LLM call is "not reported" (the `llm_request` mark carries a message count, fdbench wants tokens).
- TTS first audio n = 23 of 30: fast-path and filler turns send no TTS-start mark.
- The cable path excludes the Pi speaker hop and echo cancellation, and the laptop was on Wi-Fi: the numbers are not Pi-hardware or echo-path numbers.
- fdbench picks the stage-test phrases at random per run; pin them with `--phrases-dir` before any before/after claim. The stage test ran once (n = 30).
- Tools were on; Donna's 1.02 s median was tools off and early LLM calls, so do not compare directly.
- The judge is a Haiku 4.5 stand-in (n = 12 per group, one judge); v1 interruption rating 4.0/5.

- Ideas borrowed from the other teams but not built: a documented `BENCHMARK_MODE` (tools off) for fair runs; a scenario runner for quiet speaker, rapid turns, self-correction, long utterance; Pi resource sampling attached to a run; boot/health sound cues.

## F. Gotchas for whoever runs the stack next
- `HARNESS=pi make up` fails with "port 8780 in use" because the Pi services start at boot; only the laptop part is needed.
- `pkill -f "doc_chat --mic"` kills your own shell (exit 144). Use `pgrep -f "[d]oc_chat --mic" | xargs kill`.
- `PULSE_SOURCE` and `PULSE_SINK` are ignored by the ALSA path; move streams with `pactl move-source-output <id> fdb_to_bot.monitor` and `pactl set-default-sink fdb_from_bot`, then restore the default sink.
- Wi-Fi power save on the Pi could not be confirmed after `make pi-tune` (the governor reads `performance`).
- A 12-round fdbench run takes about 80 minutes and about $0.20 of judge calls; hold the laptop awake (`systemd-inhibit`).
