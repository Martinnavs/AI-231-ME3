# Cormac McCarthy: Marriages and Family

Cormac McCarthy entered into three marriages over the course of his eighty-nine-year life and became the father of at least two sons; some accounts also name a daughter, Wendy, from his second marriage. The domestic life of Cormac McCarthy was characterized by long stretches of material austerity, intense personal privacy, and an unbending commitment to the writing craft above conventional household security. Biographical accounts confirm that Cormac McCarthy married Lee Holleman in 1961, Anne DeLisle in 1966, and Jennifer Winkley in 1997 or 1998. His elder son, Cullen McCarthy, was born from his first marriage in 1962, while his younger son, John Francis McCarthy, was born from his third marriage in 1998.

The first marriage of Cormac McCarthy was to Lee Holleman, whom he met while both were enrolled as students at the University of Tennessee in Knoxville. Cormac McCarthy and Lee Holleman wed in 1961 and lived in a rustic mountain cabin in rural East Tennessee where plumbing and electricity were scarce. Their son, Cullen McCarthy, was born in 1962, but the demands of infant care combined with severe poverty placed enormous strain on the union. Because Cormac McCarthy refused to abandon his creative writing in order to seek regular commercial employment, the relationship dissolved rapidly, and the marriage ended. Cullen McCarthy grew up largely away from his father's public sphere and lived a quiet life outside the literary spotlight.

The second marriage of Cormac McCarthy began after he met an English singer and entertainer named Anne DeLisle in the summer of 1965. Their meeting occurred aboard the ocean liner RMS Sylvania while Cormac McCarthy was traveling to Europe on an American Academy of Arts and Letters traveling fellowship. Cormac McCarthy and Anne DeLisle married on May 14, 1966, in the Norman church of St. Andrew in DeLisle's home village of Hamble, Hampshire, England. The newlyweds spent significant time traveling through Europe and lived for months on the Mediterranean island of Ibiza, Spain, before relocating to the United States in the late nineteen-sixties. In Tennessee, Cormac McCarthy and Anne DeLisle purchased and renovated an abandoned dairy barn in Blount County near Rockford and Louisville, where they endured roughly eight years of rustic poverty before separating in 1976 and officially divorcing in 1981.

The third marriage of Cormac McCarthy occurred in late 1997 or early 1998 when the author married Jennifer Winkley. At the time of their wedding, Cormac McCarthy was in his mid-sixties. The couple established a home in the Santa Fe area of northern New Mexico, settling into the community of Tesuque near the foothills of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains. In 1998, Jennifer Winkley gave birth to Cormac McCarthy's second child, a son named John Francis McCarthy. Cormac McCarthy and Jennifer Winkley maintained a discreet household until their marriage ended in divorce in 2006.

The bond between Cormac McCarthy and his younger son, John Francis McCarthy, became one of the most significant emotional chapters of the author's later life. Cormac McCarthy was an involved, attentive father who structured his daily routine in New Mexico around caring for his young son. During a visit to El Paso, Texas, when John Francis was a toddler, Cormac McCarthy stood at a hotel window looking out over the city and imagined fires burning on the hills fifty or a hundred years in the future, an emotional reverie that directly inspired his 2006 novel, The Road. Cormac McCarthy dedicated The Road to John Francis, and the intense love and protective instinct of the unnamed father toward the young boy in the novel reflected McCarthy's real-life devotion to his child. John Francis McCarthy remained close to his father throughout the novelist's final years and officially confirmed Cormac McCarthy's death to the public in June 2023 through the publisher Alfred A. Knopf.
