# The Counselor

The Counselor is an original screenplay written by Cormac McCarthy and directed as a feature film by Ridley Scott. Cormac McCarthy wrote The Counselor as his first spec script specifically created for theatrical feature film production rather than adapted from an existing novel. Alfred A. Knopf published Cormac McCarthy's screenplay for The Counselor in book form in October 2013 to coincide with the movie's release. 20th Century Fox distributed the film, which Ridley Scott dedicated to his younger brother, filmmaker Tony Scott, who had died during pre-production in 2012.

The cast of The Counselor was led by Michael Fassbender in the title role of the unnamed Southwestern lawyer. Penélope Cruz starred in The Counselor as Laura, the Counselor's deeply religious and unsuspecting fiancée. Cameron Diaz played Malkina, a ruthlessly sociopathic mastermind who keeps pet cheetahs, while Javier Bardem portrayed Reiner, an eccentric nightclub owner and drug middleman. Brad Pitt appeared in The Counselor as Westray, a cynical philosophical fixer who warns the Counselor about the unforgiving brutality of Mexican cartels. Supporting roles in The Counselor were played by Bruno Ganz as an Amsterdam diamond dealer, Rosie Perez as an incarcerated client named Ruth, and Rubén Blades as an elite cartel figure named Jefe.

The plot of The Counselor follows an affluent defense attorney who decides to invest in a one-time narcotics shipment from Ciudad Juárez to Chicago to maintain his lavish lifestyle and marry Laura. The Counselor partners with Reiner and Westray to invest in a twenty-million-dollar cocaine consignment concealed within a sewage truck. The Counselor also agrees to post bail for the teenage son of his imprisoned client Ruth, oblivious to the fact that the young man is a high-speed cartel courier carrying a key wire component. Malkina arranges for a mercenary to decapitate the courier with a wire strung across a desert highway, hijacking the sewage truck and framing the Counselor, Reiner, and Westray for the theft.

The climax of The Counselor depicts the total destruction of everyone connected to the intercepted shipment by vengeful cartel enforcers. Cartel hitmen ambush and murder Reiner in Texas, while rival operatives abduct Laura from an airport parking lot, decapitate her, and discard her body in a municipal dump. Westray flees to London with millions in stolen cash, but Malkina tracks him down and executes him using an automated wire garrote known as a bolito before seizing his encrypted bank codes. The Counselor flees to a squalid border hotel in Mexico and calls Jefe for mercy, only to receive a calm lecture explaining that tragic choices create permanent realities that cannot be undone. The Counselor ends in ruin, showing the lawyer weeping inconsolably while Malkina leisurely prepares her next financial maneuvers.

The primary themes of The Counselor revolve around human greed, moral complicity, sexual decadence, and the absolute irreversibility of fatal choices. Cormac McCarthy structures The Counselor as a harsh moral fable warning that individuals cannot dabble in monstrous enterprises without being consumed by them. The Counselor presents the international drug trade not merely as a criminal enterprise, but as an omnipotent, predatory system that eradicates conventional human empathy.

The critical reception of The Counselor was sharply divided upon its theatrical debut in October 2013. Many prominent critics dismissed The Counselor as an overly verbose, pretentious, and disjointed thriller whose stylized monologues failed to mesh with Ridley Scott's glossy visual aesthetic. Audience reaction to The Counselor was similarly hostile, resulting in an unfavorable D grade from CinemaScore exit polling. The Counselor struggled at the domestic box office, earning roughly ten million dollars during its opening weekend and grossing seventy-one million dollars worldwide against a twenty-five-million-dollar production budget.

The retrospective reputation of The Counselor has improved significantly since its initial release, cultivating a passionate cult following among cinephiles and literary scholars. Later defenders of The Counselor have praised Cormac McCarthy's unapologetic bleakness, dark humor, and willingness to subvert the established conventions of Hollywood crime cinema. Modern critics frequently cite Cameron Diaz's chilling performance and the film's unflinching examination of existential dread as highlights of Ridley Scott and Cormac McCarthy's collaboration.
