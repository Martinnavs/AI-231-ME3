# All the Pretty Horses

## John Grady Cole and Departure from Texas
All the Pretty Horses is a 1992 novel by Cormac McCarthy that served as the opening volume of the Border Trilogy and established the author as a major literary celebrity. Published in May 1992 by Alfred A. Knopf under the editorial guidance of Gary Fisketjon, the book sold more than 180,000 hardcover copies within its first six months and became McCarthy's first national bestseller. The novel centers on sixteen-year-old John Grady Cole, an exceptionally talented horseman living on his family's ancestral cattle ranch near San Angelo, Texas. Following the death of his grandfather in 1949 and his mother's decision to sell the land, John Grady Cole realizes that the traditional Texas cattle economy is being dismantled by postwar urbanization. Refusing to abandon his equestrian vocation, John Grady Cole convinces his close companion Lacey Rawlins to ride their horses south across the Rio Grande in search of authentic cowboy work in Mexico.

## The Journey into Mexico and the Hacienda
The journey into Mexico in All the Pretty Horses begins with adventure before descending into violence and legal peril. Shortly after crossing the border, John Grady Cole and Lacey Rawlins are trailed and joined by Jimmy Blevins, an impulsive thirteen-year-old runaway riding an magnificent, oversized bay horse that he likely stole. During a ferocious desert lightning storm, Jimmy Blevins becomes terrified, abandons his horse and firearms, and loses his possessions in a flash flood. Blevins spots his stolen horse tied behind a house in the Mexican town of Encantada and recklessly retrieves it, triggering an armed pursuit that separates Blevins from John Grady Cole and Rawlins. Leaving Blevins behind, John Grady Cole and Lacey Rawlins travel south to Coahuila, where they secure employment at the sprawling Hacienda de la Purísima Concepción owned by wealthy cattle baron Don Héctor Rocha y Villareal.

## Romance, Arrest, and Saltillo Prison
John Grady Cole's fortunes at the Mexican hacienda rise and fall rapidly due to romantic passion and social hierarchy. Don Héctor promotes John Grady Cole after the young Texan displays miraculous skill breaking and training wild horses, but John Grady soon begins an illicit romance with Don Héctor's daughter, Alejandra. Alejandra's great-aunt Dueña Alfonsa discovers the relationship and warns John Grady Cole that strict Mexican social conventions and family honor will never tolerate a marriage between an impoverished American laborer and an aristocratic daughter. When Don Héctor learns of the betrayal, he contacts Mexican state police, who arrest John Grady Cole and Lacey Rawlins and transport them to Encantada. In Encantada, the boys discover that Jimmy Blevins returned to recover his gun and killed a town constable, leading the corrupt local police captain to summarily execute Blevins beside a deserted rural road.

## Climax and Return to Texas
The harrowing climax of All the Pretty Horses unfolds inside the violent state penitentiary at Saltillo, Mexico. Incarcerated among hardened criminals, John Grady Cole is ambushed in the mess hall and barely survives by killing a hired prison assassin in a brutal knife duel. Dueña Alfonsa secures the release of John Grady Cole and Lacey Rawlins by using her family influence, demanding in return that Alejandra permanently sever all ties with John Grady. Lacey Rawlins returns to Texas by bus, but John Grady Cole rides back to Encantada, takes the corrupt police captain hostage at gunpoint, and successfully reclaims his and Rawlins' confiscated horses. After a heartbreaking final conversation with Alejandra confirms that she will not defy her family, John Grady Cole rides back across the Rio Grande into Texas alone, wandering forward into an uncertain future.

## Literary Awards Won
Literary awards won by All the Pretty Horses in 1992 catapulted Cormac McCarthy to the highest ranks of American fiction. The novel was awarded the prestigious National Book Award for Fiction in 1992, marking McCarthy's first major national literary prize. That same year, All the Pretty Horses received the National Book Critics Circle Award for Fiction in 1992, honoring its lyrical prose and tragic vision. The Texas Institute of Letters further recognized the novel's regional excellence by granting McCarthy the Jesse H. Jones Award for Best Work of Fiction in 1992.

## The 2000 Film Adaptation
The 2000 film adaptation of All the Pretty Horses was directed by Billy Bob Thornton and produced by Columbia Pictures in partnership with Miramax Films. The film featured an ensemble cast starring Matt Damon as John Grady Cole, Henry Thomas as Lacey Rawlins, Penélope Cruz as Alejandra, and Lucas Black as Jimmy Blevins. Billy Bob Thornton initially assembled an epic cut running over three hours with an evocative acoustic musical score composed by Daniel Lanois. Miramax executive Harvey Weinstein forced severe cuts to transform the film into a conventional romantic drama, trimming the runtime to 117 minutes and replacing the score with music by Marty Stuart. The truncated theatrical version proved to be a critical failure and a commercial box-office disaster upon its release in December 2000, prompting Matt Damon to publicly complain that deleting over a third of the film destroyed its narrative power.
