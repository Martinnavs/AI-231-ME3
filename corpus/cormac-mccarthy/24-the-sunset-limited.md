# The Sunset Limited

The Sunset Limited is a dramatic work written by Cormac McCarthy and subtitled A Novel in Dramatic Form. Cormac McCarthy wrote The Sunset Limited as a two-character stage play exploring the clash between religious faith and existential nihilism. Alfred A. Knopf published The Sunset Limited in book form in October 2006, while Vintage Books issued subsequent trade paperback editions.

The stage premiere of The Sunset Limited took place at the Steppenwolf Theatre in Chicago on May 18, 2006. The Steppenwolf production of The Sunset Limited was directed by Sheldon Patinkin and featured Austin Pendleton as White and Freeman Coffey as Black. The theatrical staging of The Sunset Limited earned critical praise for its stripped-down staging and relentless intellectual momentum.

The setting of The Sunset Limited is confined entirely to a sparse, run-down tenement apartment in Harlem, New York City. Cormac McCarthy restricts the physical space of The Sunset Limited to a single room containing a table, two chairs, a stove, and a heavily chained door. The confined apartment setting of The Sunset Limited emphasizes the trapped, claustrophobic nature of the dialogue and prevents either character from escaping the intellectual debate.

The characters in The Sunset Limited are known only by their designations Black and White. Black in The Sunset Limited is an African American ex-convict who found evangelical Christian faith while serving time in prison for murder. White in The Sunset Limited is a white, tenured college professor of literature who has completely lost faith in art, culture, and human purpose. Black and White in The Sunset Limited represent polarized worldviews, with Black advocating for divine grace and communal love while White defends intellectual despair and death.

The plot of The Sunset Limited begins moments after Black saves White from jumping in front of a high-speed commuter train named the Sunset Limited on a New York subway platform. Black brings White back to his Harlem apartment to share a meal of soul food and prevent White from attempting suicide again. Black locks his apartment door and uses humor, scripture, and personal anecdotes to persuade White that human existence holds intrinsic value. White gradually drops his polite defenses and reveals an absolute hatred for humanity, insisting that oblivion is the only rational conclusion for an enlightened thinker. White eventually demands to be let out, leaving Black kneeling on the floor in tears, crying out to God yet reaffirming his belief.

The central themes of The Sunset Limited examine the struggle between divine belief and mortal futility, the limits of secular education, and the human responsibility to care for others. Cormac McCarthy uses The Sunset Limited to question whether Western high culture can provide meaning when faced with the reality of suffering and mortality. The Sunset Limited also dramatizes the tension between compassion and free will, demonstrating that love cannot compel an unwilling soul to choose life.

The television adaptation of The Sunset Limited was produced by HBO and premiered on February 12, 2011. Tommy Lee Jones directed and executive produced the film version of The Sunset Limited, working directly from a screenplay written by Cormac McCarthy. Tommy Lee Jones also starred as the suicidal professor White, playing opposite Samuel L. Jackson as the devout former inmate Black. The 2011 HBO film of The Sunset Limited received critical acclaim for the magnetic chemistry between its two lead actors and its faithful translation of Cormac McCarthy's stage dialogue to the screen.
