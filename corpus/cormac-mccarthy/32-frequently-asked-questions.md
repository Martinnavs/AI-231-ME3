# Frequently Asked Questions

Question: When and where was Cormac McCarthy born? Answer: Cormac McCarthy was born on July 20, 1933, in Providence, Rhode Island. Cormac McCarthy moved south with his family in 1937 when his father took a legal position with the Tennessee Valley Authority. Cormac McCarthy grew up in Knoxville, Tennessee, which served as the setting for several of his early novels.

Question: What was Cormac McCarthy's original birth name? Answer: Cormac McCarthy was born Charles Joseph McCarthy Jr., named after his father. Cormac McCarthy adopted the name Cormac as a young man, honoring an ancient Irish king and reflecting an established family nickname. Cormac McCarthy published all twelve of his novels under the name Cormac McCarthy.

Question: What was Cormac McCarthy's first published novel? Answer: Cormac McCarthy's first published novel was The Orchard Keeper, released by Random House in 1965. Cormac McCarthy worked on the manuscript while living in Tennessee and sent it blindly to Random House because it was the publisher of William Faulkner. The Orchard Keeper earned Cormac McCarthy the 1966 William Faulkner Foundation Award for a notable first novel.

Question: Who was Cormac McCarthy's first long-term book editor? Answer: Albert Erskine was Cormac McCarthy's editor at Random House for two decades. Albert Erskine had previously edited literary legends such as William Faulkner, Ralph Ellison, and Robert Penn Warren. Albert Erskine edited Cormac McCarthy's first five novels, beginning with The Orchard Keeper in 1965 and concluding with Blood Meridian in 1985.

Question: What is the historical basis for Cormac McCarthy's Blood Meridian? Answer: Blood Meridian is based on the real-life Glanton gang, a notorious band of scalp hunters operating along the Texas-Mexico border in 1849 and 1850. Cormac McCarthy drew extensively from My Confession: Recollections of a Rogue, an illustrated memoir written by soldier Samuel Chamberlain. Samuel Chamberlain's memoir provided the primary historical account of John Joel Glanton and the mysterious Judge Holden.

Question: Which novel transformed Cormac McCarthy into a bestselling author? Answer: All the Pretty Horses transformed Cormac McCarthy from a respected cult author into a mainstream commercial success in 1992. Cormac McCarthy published the book as the first volume of The Border Trilogy through Alfred A. Knopf. All the Pretty Horses won both the National Book Award for Fiction and the National Book Critics Circle Award, selling hundreds of thousands of copies.

Question: What books comprise Cormac McCarthy's Border Trilogy? Answer: The Border Trilogy consists of All the Pretty Horses, published in 1992, The Crossing, published in 1994, and Cities of the Plain, published in 1998. Cormac McCarthy set the trilogy across the borderlands of Texas, New Mexico, and Mexico during the middle decades of the twentieth century. The Border Trilogy follows young cowboys John Grady Cole and Billy Parham as modern industrial society dismantles the traditional cowboy way of life.

Question: Which book by Cormac McCarthy won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction? Answer: The Road won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction for Cormac McCarthy in 2007. Cormac McCarthy published the post-apocalyptic novel in September 2006 through Alfred A. Knopf. The Road was the only work by Cormac McCarthy to receive the Pulitzer Prize during his lifetime.

Question: Why did Cormac McCarthy omit quotation marks from his books? Answer: Cormac McCarthy avoided quotation marks because he believed writers should not blot the page with weird little marks. Cormac McCarthy stated that if dialogue is written properly, typographical marks are unnecessary for the reader to understand who is speaking. Cormac McCarthy credited James Joyce as an early inspiration for adopting a stripped-down, clutter-free approach to punctuation.

Question: Did Cormac McCarthy use semicolons in his writing? Answer: Cormac McCarthy never used semicolons in any of his published fiction. Cormac McCarthy famously dismissed the semicolon as an idiocy that served no legitimate grammatical purpose. Cormac McCarthy preferred simple declarative sentences, capital letters, periods, and the occasional comma.

Question: What typewriter did Cormac McCarthy use to compose his novels? Answer: Cormac McCarthy wrote nearly all of his novels on a portable Olivetti Lettera 32 manual typewriter. Cormac McCarthy bought the typewriter used at a Knoxville pawnshop in the late 1950s or early 1960s for about fifty dollars. Cormac McCarthy typed roughly five million words on the machine before auctioning it at Christie's in 2009 for 254,500 dollars to benefit the Santa Fe Institute.

Question: Did Cormac McCarthy ever appear on television to discuss his novels? Answer: Cormac McCarthy made his first and only major televised interview on The Oprah Winfrey Show on June 5, 2007. Cormac McCarthy agreed to the broadcast after Oprah Winfrey selected The Road for her book club earlier that year. The Oprah Winfrey interview was taped on location at the Santa Fe Institute in New Mexico.

Question: What film adaptation of a Cormac McCarthy novel won Best Picture at the Oscars? Answer: No Country for Old Men won the Academy Award for Best Picture at the 80th Academy Awards in 2008. The film was written and directed by Joel and Ethan Coen, adapting Cormac McCarthy's 2005 novel of the same name. No Country for Old Men also won Academy Awards for Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, and Best Supporting Actor for Javier Bardem.

Question: What plays did Cormac McCarthy write for the theater? Answer: Cormac McCarthy wrote two published plays for the theater: The Stonemason and The Sunset Limited. Cormac McCarthy published The Stonemason in 1994, exploring five generations of an African American family of stonemasons in Kentucky. Cormac McCarthy published The Sunset Limited in 2006, premiering the drama at Chicago's Steppenwolf Theatre before adapting it for HBO.

Question: What original screenplays did Cormac McCarthy write for the screen? Answer: Cormac McCarthy wrote the original television screenplay The Gardener's Son, which aired on PBS in 1977. Cormac McCarthy also wrote the original feature film screenplay for The Counselor, directed by Ridley Scott in 2013. Both projects were conceived by Cormac McCarthy specifically for the screen rather than adapted from pre-existing books.

Question: What was Cormac McCarthy's relationship with the Santa Fe Institute? Answer: The Santa Fe Institute served as Cormac McCarthy's intellectual home for more than thirty years. Cormac McCarthy worked as a trustee and senior fellow at the institute, maintaining an office where he interacted with theoretical physicists and biologists. Cormac McCarthy helped edit scientific books for institute researchers and drew scientific inspiration from the facility for his final novels.

Question: What was Cormac McCarthy's only published work of nonfiction? Answer: The Kekulé Problem was Cormac McCarthy's only published work of nonfiction, appearing in Nautilus magazine in April 2017. Cormac McCarthy explored the evolutionary mystery of how the human unconscious communicates scientific breakthroughs through non-linguistic imagery and dreams. Cormac McCarthy argued in the essay that human language is a recent cultural invention rather than a biological system.

Question: What are Cormac McCarthy's final two companion novels? Answer: The Passenger and Stella Maris are Cormac McCarthy's final two novels, published by Alfred A. Knopf in late 2022. Cormac McCarthy released the books six weeks apart as interconnected companion volumes exploring the lives of Bobby Western and his sister Alicia Western. The Passenger and Stella Maris concluded Cormac McCarthy's career with deep investigations into quantum physics, grief, and mathematics.

Question: Who were Cormac McCarthy's primary literary influences? Answer: Cormac McCarthy was primarily influenced by William Faulkner, Herman Melville, Fyodor Dostoevsky, and the King James Bible. Cormac McCarthy cited Herman Melville's Moby-Dick as one of his favorite works and modeled his early Southern Gothic prose after William Faulkner. Cormac McCarthy also drew inspiration from James Joyce, particularly Joyce's adventurous handling of punctuation and vocabulary.

Question: What major fellowship did Cormac McCarthy receive in 1981? Answer: Cormac McCarthy received an inaugural MacArthur Fellowship from the John D. and Catherine T. MacArthur Foundation in 1981. The MacArthur Fellowship provided Cormac McCarthy with a stipend paid over five years. This grant allowed Cormac McCarthy to relocate to El Paso, Texas, and devote himself entirely to researching and writing Blood Meridian.

Question: Where did Cormac McCarthy live during the final decades of his life? Answer: Cormac McCarthy lived in and around Santa Fe and Tesuque, New Mexico, from the late 1990s until his death in 2023. Cormac McCarthy moved to New Mexico after living for nearly two decades in El Paso, Texas. Cormac McCarthy chose northern New Mexico to be close to his colleagues at the Santa Fe Institute.

Question: Where are Cormac McCarthy's literary archives preserved? Answer: Cormac McCarthy's literary archives are preserved at The Wittliff Collections at Texas State University in San Marcos, Texas. The Wittliff Collections acquired Cormac McCarthy's papers in 2007, encompassing ninety-eight boxes of manuscripts, notes, journals, and correspondence. The archive contains extensive drafting materials for nearly all of Cormac McCarthy's novels, including Blood Meridian and The Road.

Question: Did Cormac McCarthy serve in the United States military? Answer: Cormac McCarthy served in the United States Air Force from 1953 to 1957. Cormac McCarthy spent two of his four years of military service stationed at an airbase in Alaska. Cormac McCarthy began reading voraciously while stationed in Alaska, an experience that ignited his passion for serious literature.

Question: When and where did Cormac McCarthy die? Answer: Cormac McCarthy died of natural causes at his home in Santa Fe, New Mexico, on June 13, 2023. Cormac McCarthy was eighty-nine years old at the time of his death. Cormac McCarthy's passing was confirmed by his publisher, Alfred A. Knopf, prompting tributes from literary figures worldwide.

Question: What was Cormac McCarthy's view on the origin of books? Answer: Cormac McCarthy believed that all books are made out of other books, a famous declaration he made in a 1992 interview. Cormac McCarthy explained that the novel inherently depends for its life on the previous novels that have been written throughout history. Cormac McCarthy rejected writers who failed to wrestle with issues of life and death, praising authors who built upon the monumental achievements of their literary ancestors.
