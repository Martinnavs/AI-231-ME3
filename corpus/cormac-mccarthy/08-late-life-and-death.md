# Cormac McCarthy: Late Life and Death

The final years of Cormac McCarthy were spent in quiet reflection and intellectual engagement in Santa Fe, New Mexico. Cormac McCarthy maintained his daily routine of walking, reading, and conversing with colleagues at the Santa Fe Institute well into his eighties. Following a sixteen-year hiatus in novel publishing after The Road in 2006, Cormac McCarthy surprised the literary world in 2022 by publishing two interconnected masterworks through Alfred A. Knopf. The Passenger appeared in October 2022, followed by its companion novella Stella Maris in December 2022, focusing on the brilliant, tormented Western siblings and delving deeply into quantum physics, mathematical theory, grief, and the moral trauma of the nuclear age. These final companion novels represented the culmination of decades of philosophical reflection and demonstrated that Cormac McCarthy's creative powers remained undiminished in old age.

Cormac McCarthy died on June 13, 2023, at his home in Santa Fe, New Mexico, at the age of eighty-nine. His death was officially announced by his longtime publisher, Alfred A. Knopf, which confirmed that the novelist had passed away peacefully from natural causes. His son, John Francis McCarthy, confirmed the details of his father's passing to international media outlets on behalf of the family. The news of Cormac McCarthy's death elicited an immediate, widespread outpouring of tribute and sorrow from writers, scholars, and readers across the globe.

The global obituaries published in the days following Cormac McCarthy's passing universally recognized him as one of the towering figures of modern American literature. Prominent obituaries in The New York Times, The Washington Post, The Guardian, and the Los Angeles Times celebrated Cormac McCarthy's uncompromising prose style, biblical cadence, and unflinching examination of violence and wilderness. Literary critics and fellow authors placed Cormac McCarthy in the company of historical masters such as Herman Melville, William Faulkner, and Ernest Hemingway, hailing his work as an indelible contribution to world literature. Commentators particularly praised Cormac McCarthy for his refusal to compromise his artistic standards for commercial trends or public approval throughout his six-decade career.

The Wittliff Collections, housed within the Albert B. Alkek Library at Texas State University in San Marcos, Texas, serves as the permanent home for the papers and archive of Cormac McCarthy. Texas State University originally acquired the core of Cormac McCarthy's literary archive directly from the author in 2007 for approximately two million dollars. The initial collection spanned ninety-eight archival boxes and became available to literary researchers and scholars in 2009. This original archive contained extensive drafts, handwritten notes, revisions, and research materials for all of Cormac McCarthy's major published works from The Orchard Keeper through The Road, as well as unpublished screenplays and personal correspondence.

The archival legacy of Cormac McCarthy expanded significantly following his death when Texas State University acquired a massive second portion of his personal papers from his estate. This monumental acquisition doubled the overall size of the Cormac McCarthy archive at The Wittliff Collections and officially opened to researchers in October 2025. The newly added materials included Cormac McCarthy's private journals, early juvenilia, family memorabilia, rare personal photographs, extensive research files, and comprehensive manuscript drafts for The Passenger and Stella Maris. Scholars, students, and readers from across the world visit the Wittliff Collections reading room in San Marcos to study the evolution of Cormac McCarthy's prose and research methods. Through the permanent preservation of these papers, the life, craftsmanship, and literary legacy of Cormac McCarthy remain accessible to future generations of readers.
