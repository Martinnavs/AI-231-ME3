# Cities of the Plain

## Uniting the Two Heroes
Cities of the Plain is the 1998 novel by Cormac McCarthy that brings the Border Trilogy to its poignant conclusion. Published in May 1998 by Alfred A. Knopf, the work originated in the mid-1980s as an unproduced spec screenplay before McCarthy decided to adapt and expand the concept into prose. The novel unites the protagonists of the two earlier volumes, pairing twenty-six-year-old John Grady Cole from All the Pretty Horses with twenty-eight-year-old Billy Parham from The Crossing. Set in the autumn of 1952, the story finds John Grady Cole and Billy Parham employed as ranch hands on a cattle spread south of Alamogordo, New Mexico, managed by an aging rancher named Mac McGovern. Working side by side against the pressures of an extended drought and looming military land acquisitions, the two young horsemen forge a close bond rooted in mutual respect for horses and cowboy labor.

## The Conflict in Ciudad Juárez
John Grady Cole's quest in Ciudad Juárez forms the dramatic catalyst that drives the tragic plot of Cities of the Plain. During weekend visits across the border with fellow ranch hands, John Grady Cole visits an illicit brothel in Ciudad Juárez and falls deeply in love with Magdalena, an exploited young woman who suffers from epilepsy. Driven by his enduring chivalric ideals, John Grady Cole resolves to purchase Magdalena's freedom, marry her, and move her to an abandoned stone cabin on the New Mexico ranch that he painstakingly repairs. Billy Parham recognizes the immense danger of trying to extract Magdalena from the criminal syndicate that controls her and urges John Grady to abandon the plan. Despite his pragmatic warnings, Billy Parham remains loyal to his companion, agreeing to serve as a supportive confidant as John Grady plans Magdalena's escape across the border.

The tragic climax of Cities of the Plain erupts when Magdalena's escape plan is uncovered by Eduardo, the calculating, menacing manager of the Juárez brothel. Eduardo arranges for Magdalena to be intercepted and murdered near the border crossing, leaving her lifeless body in an alleyway to crush John Grady Cole's hopes. Devastated by grief and vengeance, John Grady Cole crosses into Ciudad Juárez to confront Eduardo directly inside an abandoned warehouse. John Grady Cole and Eduardo engage in a lethal duel with knives, resulting in Eduardo's death but leaving John Grady mortally wounded with severe lacerations. Billy Parham searches desperately for his friend, finds John Grady dying in the Mexican streets, and cradles him in his arms during his final breaths before transporting his body back across the border for burial.

## The Philosophical Epilogue
The philosophical epilogue of Cities of the Plain shifts the narrative fifty years into the future to provide a haunting meditation on the vanishing Western past. Set in the year 2002, the epilogue presents Billy Parham as an impoverished, seventy-eight-year-old drifter sleeping beneath highway overpasses in a modernized Southwest that has forgotten the cowboy trade. Billy Parham encounters a mysterious traveler who engages him in an extended, dreamlike dialogue regarding fate, memory, and the unseen forces shaping human existence. The traveler's philosophical discourse suggests that life is an endless tapestry of stories wherein individual deeds are swallowed by time, leaving memory as the only frail bridge to the lost world. Cities of the Plain concludes with an elderly Billy Parham finding shelter with a charitable Mexican family, dreaming of his deceased brother Boyd and weeping for the comrades of his youth.

## Critical Reception and Assessment
Critical reception of Cities of the Plain in 1998 was largely respectful, with reviewers acknowledging Cormac McCarthy's mastery of Western dialect and elegiac atmosphere. Several literary critics observed that the novel's fast-moving, dialogue-heavy structure reflected its origins as a 1980s screenplay, making it less stylistically expansive than All the Pretty Horses or The Crossing. Reviewers widely praised the authentic camaraderie between John Grady Cole and Billy Parham, highlighting their conversations around ranch campfires as some of McCarthy's most tender character work. Literary scholars frequently single out the 2002 epilogue as the philosophical pinnacle of the entire Border Trilogy, commending McCarthy's poetic examination of historical obsolescence. Overall, Cities of the Plain stands as a fitting and sorrowful capstone to the trilogy, completing McCarthy's portrait of the closing American frontier.
