# The Passenger and Stella Maris

The Passenger and Stella Maris are two interconnected novels written by Cormac McCarthy and published in late 2022 by Alfred A. Knopf. The arrival of The Passenger and Stella Maris marked Cormac McCarthy's first new fiction in sixteen years, following his 2006 Pulitzer Prize-winning novel The Road. Alfred A. Knopf released The Passenger on October 25, 2022, and issued Stella Maris six weeks later on December 6, 2022. The publisher offered both books individually and simultaneously made them available together in a paired collector's box set.

The protagonists of The Passenger and Stella Maris are Bobby Western and his younger sister Alicia Western. Bobby Western in The Passenger is a brilliant former physics student turned deep-sea salvage diver and former Formula Two racing driver who survived a devastating coma. Alicia Western in Stella Maris is a twenty-year-old doctoral candidate in pure mathematics at the University of Chicago who experiences intense hallucinations brought on by paranoid schizophrenia. The Western siblings in both novels are haunted by the legacy of their late father, a prominent nuclear physicist who worked with J. Robert Oppenheimer on the Manhattan Project at Oak Ridge to construct the atomic bomb. Bobby and Alicia Western also share a tragic, mutual romantic obsession that isolates them from ordinary human relationships.

The plot of The Passenger is set primarily along the Gulf Coast and New Orleans in 1980. The narrative begins when Bobby Western dives into a sunken private jet in the Gulf of Mexico and discovers nine dead passengers strapped into their seats with the flight recorder and a tenth passenger inexplicably missing. Bobby Western soon finds himself surveilled, harassed, and targeted by shadowy government agents who freeze his bank accounts and confiscate his passport. Bobby Western flees across the country, visiting old acquaintances in Idaho and East Tennessee while mourning the memory of Alicia, who took her own life in 1972. The fugitive plot of The Passenger gradually dissolves into an existential meditation as Bobby lives out his final days in self-imposed exile in Spain.

The plot and structure of Stella Maris take place eight years prior to The Passenger, set in the autumn of 1972. Stella Maris is constructed entirely as a series of verbatim dialogue transcripts between Alicia Western and her treating psychiatrist, Dr. Robert Meyers, at a psychiatric hospital in Black River Falls, Wisconsin. Alicia Western checks herself into Stella Maris with forty thousand dollars in cash in a plastic bag, seeking refuge from her hallucinatory phantoms, which include a sarcastic dwarf-like figure named the Thalidomide Kid. Across seven recorded sessions, Alicia Western debates Dr. Meyers on the foundational philosophy of mathematics, quantum mechanics, the origins of language, and her unbearable grief over Bobby, who lies comatose in Europe following his racing accident. The dialogue in Stella Maris concludes with Alicia calmly preparing for her suicide, which she carries out in the nearby snowy woods shortly after the final session.

The primary themes of The Passenger and Stella Maris explore the epistemological limits of human consciousness, the moral guilt of the nuclear age, and the agonizing weight of grief. Cormac McCarthy treats the development of the atomic bomb as a catastrophic rupture that fundamentally severed humanity from the physical universe and objective reality. The Passenger and Stella Maris also probe the nature of mathematics, investigating whether numbers exist as objective truths woven into the cosmos or merely as illusions created by the human mind. The deep, doomed love between Bobby and Alicia Western anchors these cosmic abstractions in profound personal heartbreak.

The critical reception of The Passenger and Stella Maris was broadly laudatory, with reviewers praising the duology as a monumental, challenging capstone to Cormac McCarthy's career. Literary critics celebrated Cormac McCarthy's willingness to experiment with new narrative modes, contrasting the atmospheric, noir-tinged sprawl of The Passenger with the austere, dialectical purity of Stella Maris. Some reviewers noted that readers seeking a conventional mystery might find The Passenger frustrating because the submerged jet conspiracy is never solved. Nevertheless, scholars and major publications widely recognized the twin novels as an astonishing late-career triumph that deepened Cormac McCarthy's philosophical legacy.

The decision to publish The Passenger and Stella Maris together arose from their reciprocal structure and decades-long development. Cormac McCarthy had worked on drafts of The Passenger since the 1970s and 1980s, publicly previewing excerpts of the manuscript at a Santa Fe Institute event in August 2015. Cormac McCarthy realized that Bobby's sprawling journey in The Passenger required Alicia's intellectual counterweight in Stella Maris to complete the portrait of the Western family's tragedy. Alfred A. Knopf published The Passenger and Stella Maris in close succession because neither novel can be fully understood without the philosophical context, emotional revelations, and thematic resonance provided by the other.
