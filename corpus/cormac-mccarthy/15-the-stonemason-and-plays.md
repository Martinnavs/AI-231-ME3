# The Stonemason and Plays

## Composition and Publication of The Stonemason
Cormac McCarthy's five-act drama The Stonemason was written in the late 1980s, completed around 1990, and first published in book form in 1994 by The Ecco Press. Random House's Vintage Books imprint subsequently released a widely circulated paperback edition of The Stonemason in 1995. The work was a major departure for Cormac McCarthy, standing as his only extended creative work centered entirely on an African American family. Through the dramatic theatrical form, Cormac McCarthy explored generational responsibility, moral philosophy, and the sacred dignity of physical craftsmanship.

## Setting, Characters, and Plot of The Stonemason
The setting and characters of The Stonemason are anchored in Louisville, Kentucky, during the early 1970s, spanning roughly three years centered on the year 1971. The drama revolves around four generations of the Telfair family, an African American lineage of stonemasons headed by the ninety-year-old patriarch Papaw. The thirty-two-year-old protagonist and narrator, Ben Telfair, renounces university education to apprentice himself to Papaw, learning the spiritual ethics of laying stone true to gravity and the plumb line. Conflict develops when Ben Telfair's father, Big Ben, abandons hand craftsmanship in favor of modern speculative construction contracts, accumulating ruinous bank debts that threaten the ancestral family home with foreclosure. Following the death of Ben Telfair's young daughter Melissa and the descent of his nephew Soldier into drug crime, the family's material stability collapses, leaving Ben Telfair to preserve Papaw's moral heritage in deep mourning.

## Troubled Production History of The Stonemason
The stage production history of The Stonemason is marked by complex staging requirements and high-profile cancellations. In 1991, Washington, D.C.'s Arena Stage received a fifty-thousand-dollar grant from the Kennedy Center Fund for New American Plays to produce the world premiere of The Stonemason in 1992. Arena Stage canceled the production and returned the grant money when Cormac McCarthy refused to make requested script revisions to resolve casting and structural difficulties. A subsequent attempt to mount the play during the 1997 to 1999 seasons at the McCarter Theatre in Princeton, New Jersey, was likewise abandoned prior to production. Although biographical timelines frequently state that The Stonemason was first performed in 1995, this notation refers to minor regional readings and workshop presentations, as the drama never received a definitive major theatrical staging.

## The Sunset Limited and Theatrical Dialogue
Cormac McCarthy's second dramatic work The Sunset Limited, subtitled A Novel in Dramatic Form, was published as a book in 2006 by Vintage Books. The play consists of an intense, real-time two-character philosophical debate staged entirely inside a sparse tenement apartment in New York City. The characters are named simply Black and White, personifying two conflicting philosophical attitudes toward faith, suffering, and the value of existence. Black is an ex-convict, recovering addict, and passionate evangelical Christian who has just physically rescued White, a suicidal college professor, from jumping in front of an oncoming subway train called the Sunset Limited.

## Staging and Film Adaptation of The Sunset Limited
The world premiere and performance history of The Sunset Limited began at the Steppenwolf Theatre Company in Chicago, opening on May 18, 2006. Directed by Sheldon Patinkin, the original Steppenwolf production starred Austin Pendleton as the suicidal professor White and Freeman Coffey as the ex-convict Black. The production transferred Off-Broadway to the 59E59 Theaters in New York City in October 2006, receiving critical acclaim for its gripping intellectual tension and sharp dialogue. Director Tommy Lee Jones adapted The Sunset Limited into an acclaimed 2011 television film for HBO, in which Tommy Lee Jones portrayed White opposite Samuel L. Jackson as Black.

## The Role of Drama in McCarthy's Corpus
Cormac McCarthy's theatrical legacy illustrates the author's persistent drive to examine existential and theological conflicts through spoken dialogue. In contrast to his novels, which immerse readers in dense environmental descriptions and sensory landscapes, Cormac McCarthy's dramatic works strip away physical scenery to focus directly on human voices. Both The Stonemason and The Sunset Limited investigate the search for spiritual meaning in an indifferent universe, contrasting sacred human labor and faith against secular despair. These theatrical dramas remain essential companion texts to Cormac McCarthy's prose masterpieces, demonstrating his versatility across narrative and dramatic mediums.
