# Adaptations in Film and Television

The works of Cormac McCarthy have inspired numerous feature film and television adaptations, reflecting the intensely visual and cinematic quality of his prose. Cormac McCarthy collaborated directly on several productions as a screenwriter, while other projects were helmed by prominent Hollywood directors including Joel and Ethan Coen, Ridley Scott, and Billy Bob Thornton. Screen adaptations of Cormac McCarthy's fiction range from intimate two-character television dramas to sweeping Oscar-winning crime epics. These screen adaptations have brought Cormac McCarthy's dark thematic vision and distinctive dialogue to global audiences across five decades.

The earliest screen work by Cormac McCarthy was The Gardener's Son, a two-hour television film broadcast on PBS in January 1977 as part of the Visions anthology series. Richard Pearce directed The Gardener's Son from an original screenplay written by Cormac McCarthy based on an actual 1876 murder in a South Carolina textile town. Brad Dourif starred as Robert McEvoy, an amputee mill hand who murders the arrogant son of the mill's wealthy owner, played by Kevin Conway. The Gardener's Son also featured supporting performances by Ned Beatty and Jerry Hardin, providing Cormac McCarthy with his first professional screenwriting credit.

The first major theatrical adaptation of Cormac McCarthy's fiction was All the Pretty Horses, released in theaters in December 2000. Billy Bob Thornton directed All the Pretty Horses from a screenplay written by Ted Tally, adapting Cormac McCarthy's 1992 National Book Award-winning novel. Matt Damon starred as the idealistic young Texas cowboy John Grady Cole, while Henry Thomas portrayed his loyal companion Lacey Rawlins. Penélope Cruz played Alejandra, the Mexican rancher's daughter whose forbidden romance with John Grady Cole sparks the story's tragic conflict, alongside Lucas Black as the reckless runaway Jimmy Blevins.

The 2007 film adaptation of No Country for Old Men stands as the most critically acclaimed and decorated movie ever made from Cormac McCarthy's writing. Joel and Ethan Coen wrote, directed, produced, and edited the feature film, faithfully translating Cormac McCarthy's 2005 border thriller to the screen. Tommy Lee Jones starred as the weary Sheriff Ed Tom Bell, Josh Brolin portrayed the fugitive welder Llewelyn Moss, and Javier Bardem gave an iconic performance as the implacable hitman Anton Chigurh. No Country for Old Men won four Academy Awards in 2008, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay for the Coen brothers, and Best Supporting Actor for Javier Bardem.

The film adaptation of The Road arrived in theaters in November 2009, directed by Australian filmmaker John Hillcoat. Joe Penhall wrote the screenplay for The Road, closely following Cormac McCarthy's 2006 Pulitzer Prize-winning novel. Viggo Mortensen delivered a harrowing performance as the dying father, while Kodi Smit-McPhee portrayed the young son carrying the fire through the post-apocalyptic wasteland. The Road also featured Charlize Theron in flashback sequences as the boy's mother, Robert Duvall as the elderly traveler Ely, and Guy Pearce as the veteran survivor who takes in the orphaned boy.

An independent short film adaptation of Cormac McCarthy's 1968 novel Outer Dark was released in 2009 by writer and director Stephen Imwalle. The fifteen-minute adaptation of Outer Dark played across the United States on the film festival circuit. Jamie Dunne starred as Rinthy Holme, a young Appalachian woman searching for the infant son taken from her at birth, while Azel James portrayed her tormented brother Culla Holme. Stephen Imwalle's adaptation of Outer Dark captured the eerie, folklore-infused dread of Cormac McCarthy's early Appalachian fiction in a concise visual format.

Cormac McCarthy adapted his 2006 stage play The Sunset Limited into a celebrated television film for HBO, which premiered on February 12, 2011. Tommy Lee Jones directed, executive produced, and starred in The Sunset Limited as White, a suicidal college professor who has lost all faith in humanity. Samuel L. Jackson co-starred in The Sunset Limited as Black, an evangelical ex-convict who rescues White from an oncoming subway train and tries to convince him of God's grace. The Sunset Limited was filmed inside a single room, earning widespread praise for the magnetic, emotionally charged performances of its two veteran leads.

The Counselor was released in October 2013 as a feature film directed by Ridley Scott, featuring an original screenplay written by Cormac McCarthy. Michael Fassbender starred in The Counselor as an unnamed Southwestern attorney who entangles himself in a high-stakes twenty-million-dollar cocaine deal. Penélope Cruz appeared as the Counselor's doomed fiancée Laura, Cameron Diaz played the sociopathic instigator Malkina, Javier Bardem portrayed the flamboyant middleman Reiner, and Brad Pitt starred as the cautious cartel fixer Westray. The Counselor polarized critics with its bleak fatalism and lengthy philosophical monologues, grossing seventy-one million dollars worldwide.

An independent feature film adaptation of Cormac McCarthy's 1973 novel Child of God premiered at the Venice Film Festival in August 2013 before its theatrical release. James Franco directed and co-wrote the film adaptation of Child of God alongside Vince Jolivette. Scott Haze delivered an intense, visceral performance as Lester Ballard, a violent, dispossessed outcast who descends into necrophilia and serial murder in the caves of East Tennessee. Child of God also featured supporting performances by Tim Blake Nelson as Sheriff Fate and Jim Parrack as Deputy Cotton.
