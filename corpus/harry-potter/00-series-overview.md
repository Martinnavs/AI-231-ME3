# Harry Potter series overview

The Harry Potter series is a set of seven fantasy novels by the British author J.K. Rowling about a boy who learns he is a wizard. The first book, Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone, was published in 1997. The last, Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows, came out in 2007. In the United States the first book is called Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone.

The series has sold more than five hundred million copies and has been translated into over eighty languages. It was turned into eight films between 2001 and 2011. It was written for children, and the books grow darker and longer as Harry grows up from eleven to seventeen.

The story follows Harry Potter, who attends Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. Over seven years he makes friends, learns magic, and faces the dark wizard Lord Voldemort, who killed his parents. The main themes are friendship, courage, loyalty, love, death, and the choice between what is right and what is easy.

The seven books in order are: Philosopher's Stone, Chamber of Secrets, Prisoner of Azkaban, Goblet of Fire, Order of the Phoenix, Half-Blood Prince, and Deathly Hallows. Each book covers one school year at Hogwarts, except the last, which takes place mostly outside school.
